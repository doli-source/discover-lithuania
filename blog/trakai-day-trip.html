🏰 Trakai · Day Trip from Vilnius

Trakai Day Trip from Vilnius — The Complete 2026 Guide

✍️ By Niv Shimoni · 📅 Updated July 2026 · ⏱ 5 min read

Trakai is 28km from Vilnius and looks like it was designed to end up on a postcard — a red-brick medieval castle rising from a lake, surrounded by wooden fishing villages and 600-year-old Karaim culture. It's the most visited day trip in Lithuania, and for good reason. Here's how to do it properly.

Distance
28 km from Vilnius
Getting there
Train — 35 min, €1.50
Time needed
Half day or full day
Best season
May – September
☕
Morning · First stop
Snapas Kava ir Desertai
Specialty coffee & bakery
★★★★½ 4.7 (155 reviews) €5–10

Before anything else, come here. Snapas is the warmest corner in Trakai — a café on Vytauto Street opened by a local woman named Laima after years of searching for a proper cup of coffee in town. Single-origin coffee, fresh-baked croissants and brownies every morning, and homemade treats from her own honeyberry orchard. The kind of place that makes you slow down immediately.

🏰
Morning · The main attraction
Trakai Island Castle
Medieval castle
★★★★½ 4.7 (18,500 reviews)

A 14th-century red-brick castle rising from the middle of Lake Galvė — Lithuania's most iconic image, and it earns it. Cross the wooden bridge, explore the museum inside, and walk the full perimeter of the island. Go early to beat the crowds and get the best light on the water.

💡 Buy tickets online in advance — queues can be long in summer.

🚣
Late morning · Don't skip this
Boat Rental on Lake Galvė
Rowboat & paddleboat rental

Rent a rowboat or pedalo right on the lakefront and paddle up to the castle from the water. The view from a boat is completely different — you see the castle the way it was meant to be seen, reflected in the lake with no crowds between you and it. One of the most memorable experiences in Lithuania, and it costs almost nothing.

🥟
Lunch · Local classic
Senoji Kibininė
Traditional Karaim kitchen
★★★★½ 4.5 (2,800 reviews) €5–15

You cannot leave Trakai without eating kibinai. These are Karaim meat pies — baked fresh from a 600-year-old recipe brought to Lithuania from Crimea. Senoji Kibininė is the best place to try them: crispy pastry, seasoned filling, and the knowledge that you're eating something that's barely changed since the 14th century.

🏛️
Afternoon · Hidden gem
Karaite Ethnographic Museum
Ethnographic museum
★★★★ 4.0 (57 reviews) €1–3

Europe's only museum dedicated to the Karaim people — a small Turkic, Old Testament-following community that Grand Duke Vytautas brought from Crimea to Trakai 600 years ago. Three rooms, 300+ artifacts: traditional dress, Eastern weapons, ceremonial coffee pots, an antique wooden cradle. A 20-minute stop that most visitors walk past and never forget once they've been inside.

🍫
Afternoon · Sweet stop
AJ Šokoladas
Chocolate & coffee shop

A sweet stop for handmade chocolates and specialty coffee in Trakai's town center. Pick up a box to take home — they travel well and make a better souvenir than anything from the castle gift shop.

🎈
Optional · Bucket list
Hot Air Balloon Ride over Trakai
Hot air balloon tour

Float over Trakai's lakes and medieval castle from above — the view is extraordinary and completely different from anything you'll see on the ground. Balloons typically fly in the early morning or late afternoon for the best light. Book well in advance, especially in summer. One of the most memorable experiences you can have in Lithuania.

💡 Book at least a few days ahead — flights depend on weather and fill up fast in peak season.

🌙 Staying overnight? Worth considering

🏨 Esperanza Lake Resort & Spa — Eleven hectares of private forest on a lake ten minutes from Trakai, built from century-old Canadian cedar. Two resident alpacas, a hammam, salt room, and sauna. One of the most beautiful places to stay in Lithuania.

Practical tips for your Trakai day trip

  • Train from Vilnius: 35 minutes, runs every hour, costs around €1.50 each way
  • Castle tickets: ~€8 adults, buy online to skip the queue
  • Best time to visit: weekday mornings — weekends get crowded fast
  • Everything is walkable — the town is small, no car needed
  • Bring cash for the boat rental and the kibinai stand
  • Half a day is enough for the castle + lunch; a full day lets you add the balloon and museum
