Behind the Scenes

The People Behind the Stories

When the people behind the stories finally gathered around one table in Vilnius

By Niv Shimoni November 24, 2026 3 min read
Niv Shimoni
Entrepreneur and founder of lithuaniadiscovery.com

On a Thursday afternoon in November, the blog stepped off the screen.

For months, lithuaniadiscovery.com has collected stories from people building Lithuania: a pastry chef, a small-town baker, a natural wine storyteller, a ceramic artist, a coffee professional, the founder of a women's gravel cycling community, a daughter sharing her mother's cepelinai recipe, cabins in the woods by a lake, and a museum reviving a lost world. On 19 November, I invited them, together with the people who helped me along the way, to BREW in Vilnius for an afternoon of food, conversation and thanks.

The right room

Every gathering needs the right room. Ours was BREW.

BREW is a specialty coffee shop and bakery on Algirdo g. 38, and one of the Vilnius cafés I keep coming back to. It was part of the blog before we ever walked in: it is on my list of the best coffee shops in the city, and its amazing and talented pastry chef, Jekaterina, told her story here.

But what made it the right room was the people behind it. Agnė and Tadas, the owners, and Erika, the manager, did more than lend us a space. They opened their café to us for the afternoon and gave the gathering a home.

The site on the wall

As guests arrived, the site and the blog ran on the wall behind us: the homepage first, then the recommended places across Lithuania, followed by the blog, one story after another. Seeing them projected in a room full of people I had invited felt very different from looking at them on a screen.

Then I told them where it all began. With my grandfather, who was born in Aukštadvaris. With a first visit almost ten years ago that turned Lithuania into my second home. And with the reason I built this site: Lithuania is best told by the people who live it.

An Israeli table in Vilnius

Next, Daniel, a chef from Vilnius and a good friend, took over the table. He cooked Israeli food. The kubaneh, a traditional Yemenite Jewish bread, had been baking in the oven all night. The hummus, by contrast, he made fresh on the spot. And alongside them: schug, a spicy paste of green chili peppers, and freshly grated tomato.

For many guests it was a first taste of Israeli food, and they loved it. Plates were refilled again and again, and people kept coming back to the table for more. Daniel will share the full story, and a recipe, in his own post, coming soon.

Before leaving, each guest took home a small gift from Israel: halva, a traditional sweet made from sesame paste.

What stayed with me

Most of all, the people: their care, their warmth, and how moved they were. Some I had known only through messages and emails until that afternoon. I came to Vilnius to say thank you, and left feeling I had received more than I gave.

Ačiū. Toda. Thank you.

To Agnė, Tadas and Erika, who opened BREW's doors and made it feel like home.
To Daniel, who brought Israel to the table, one kubaneh at a time.
To Ariela and Lina, who caught the afternoon in pictures, so we can always come back to it.
And to everyone who came, and turned a list of names into a room full of friends.

This was the first gathering. Until the next table.
