From Pencil to Clay: Agnes BR's Creative Journey

By Niv Shimoni September 2026 4 min read
Agnes BR
Vilnius-based ceramics artist, working from her studio in Trakų Vokė.
agnesbr.lt @agnesbr.lt
Guest Feature

Today I'm happy to host Agnes BR, a Vilnius-based ceramics artist, here on the blog. She's sharing her journey from finance to ceramics, and the story behind her studio and work.

I came to ceramics unexpectedly, when I took my daughter to her first pottery class. After graduating in finance and economics, I realised that office work wasn't for me. I had always been very interested in jewellery and drawing in small formats.

My teacher, the renowned ceramic artist V. Juškaitė, saw potential in me, gave me some clay and said: "Try drawing." And so, little by little, drawing found its way into ceramics. Working with gold to decorate my pieces is also a kind of reference to jewellery, which I had once dreamed of creating.

Stories in small details

When I'm not drawing on ceramics, I draw on paper. I love working with details and hiding small elements within my drawings. This is how stories emerge within a single image — when the viewer discovers those details, they can create their own story and interpretation. I draw postcards as well as larger pencil drawings, and I have even held an exhibition of these works.

Letting the work unfold

I like it when creativity comes from the heart. I don't want to plan everything in advance — sometimes I draw, shape something and simply let the work unfold on its own. My pieces often emerge quite unexpectedly, and music is a great source of inspiration. My favourite collection, "El Mar," was born while listening to ZZ Top — a collection about the sea and freedom. It has since found admirers in Italy, the US and France.

"Clay is part of nature — it isn't perfect, and its irregularities are what give a piece its charm."

Imperfection is important to me. Ceramics remind me that clay is part of nature, it isn't perfect, and its irregularities are what give a piece its charm. I no longer strive to create perfectly symmetrical vessels; I deliberately leave the marks of my hands. At the same time, I can happily spend hours working on a drawing where every single fish scale needs to be painted. I think this attention to detail is something that connects my drawings and my ceramics.

A studio inside a manor

The place where I create also inspires and challenges me. My studio is located in one of the buildings of the J. Tiškevičius manor complex in Trakų Vokė, where I am an artist-in-residence. The surroundings constantly remind me of elegance and subtlety. Perhaps that is why people are sometimes surprised that my ceramic studio is not quite a typical one: you are greeted by the scent of incense, an old piano and conversation. The pieces need to be "tried on" :)

The philosophy of the table

Sometimes clients come to create an entire tableware collection; sometimes they leave with nothing more than an idea for a single cup, which may take several weeks to bring to life. But these objects eventually become part of the rituals of the table. To slow down, to enjoy, to experience — that is my philosophy of the table and of tableware.
